# SecPO: Principled Adversarial Training for Prompt Injection Security
> arXiv cs.CR 2610.41998 (preprint, not peer reviewed). UC Berkeley, Anthropic (work done at Google DeepMind)
> 2026

Prompt injection hijacks an agent by hiding instructions in untrusted data such as emails, documents, and web pages. Meta-SecAlign, until now considered the strongest model-level defense, is trained with a DPO (direct preference optimization) loss, yet adaptive attacks have been reported to succeed against it more than 95% of the time. The authors trace part of the problem to DPO's regularization term: it keeps the model close to the original, undefended reference model, and that reference model is exactly the one that falls for injections.

The paper defines a hypothetical oracle model that answers an injected input exactly as it would answer the same input without the injection, and answers clean inputs exactly like the original model. No such model exists, but because training samples are built by adding injections to clean inputs, the original model's output distribution on the clean input is the oracle's output distribution. Building on this, the authors propose the SecPO loss, which changes only the reference term of the DPO loss, and pair it with an adversarial training pipeline that searches for strong injections during training.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41998 (full text read from the paper PDF in the authors' official repository, https://github.com/Sizhe-Chen/SecPO)

### Contribution
- Identifies, with a theoretical argument, how DPO-based defenses are pulled toward a vulnerable reference model, and derives the SecPO loss, which is implemented simply by feeding the reference model the clean input instead of the injected one. In gradient terms SecPO keeps DPO's direction but gives more weight to samples where the injection strongly shifts the original model's preference.
- Implements adversarial training with PAIR search: for each sample, 8 candidate injections per round over 30 rounds (240 attempts), keeping the strongest one for training. Attacker and judge calls are parallelized, so training Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct took 18 hours on 16 H200 GPUs.
- Releases the resulting training set with optimized injections, so other models can be protected without rerunning the search. Qwen3.6-27B was trained on it in 7 hours on 8 H200 GPUs.
- Releases code, the training set, and the defended model checkpoints.

### Evaluation
- Models: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3.6-27B (thinking disabled). Baselines are the undefended models and Meta-SecAlign; to isolate the effect of the loss, the authors also compare against the same adversarial training with a DPO loss.
- Train/test separation: training uses only Cleaned-Alpaca and the PAIR attack, while evaluation uses attacks unseen in training (Genetic, TAP, and the reinforcement-learning-based PISmith) and unseen tasks (SEP, AgentDojo, AgentDyn, DTaP).
- Qwen3.6-27B results: SEP adaptive attack success rates drop from Meta-SecAlign's 93.7% (Genetic), 90.1% (TAP), and 94.0% (PISmith) to 13.3%, 12.5%, and 1.8%. On AgentDojo, the Genetic attack from the AttackerMovesSecond paper (800 attempts) succeeds 81.3% of the time on the undefended model, 78.8% on Meta-SecAlign, and 8.8% on SecPO. Across the 1,838 DTaP scenarios the rate falls from 45.9% to 7.6%.
- Utility: the average over six benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, GPQA Diamond, GSM8K, Minerva Math, AlpacaEval2, SEP) is 88.9% for the undefended model and 89.1% for SecPO, essentially unchanged. AgentDojo utility goes from 89.7% to 90.7%, and AgentDyn stays at 71.7%.
- Ablation: adding adversarial training alone cuts attack success rates by 1.5 to 14.5 times, and swapping DPO for SecPO under the same training cuts them by another 2.4 to 4.6 times. Although trained only on text injections, the model also shows a 0% success rate against injections written on images (Repeat-After-Me).
- Reliability of the metrics: the authors found that SEP's official rule (checking whether a witness word appears) produces many false positives, so an attack counts as successful only when nine queries to three judge models all agree. Manual labels on 1,024 outputs showed zero false positives and zero false negatives for this rule. The adaptive AgentDojo evaluation uses only an 80-example subset because of its cost, which is worth keeping in mind.

#### Comment
- The idea is a one-line change to the loss, but it comes with a clear theoretical justification, and the strict separation of attack types and tasks between training and evaluation makes the results convincing. Plugging the released training set into a different recipe (SecOPD) also cut attack success rates by 2.5 to 22.5 times, which is immediately useful in practice.
- Limitations: the method assumes trusted instructions and untrusted data are explicitly separated (through a dedicated input role), so it does not cover settings without that separation or a malicious user. Extending it to reasoning models is left for future work. The attacks that still succeed are social-engineering injections framed as steps needed to finish the task; in 75 of the 140 successful DTaP attacks the original task also succeeded and the injection merely widened its scope, which hints at where the next generation of attacks is heading.
- Venue: an arXiv preprint that has not been peer reviewed. The authors are the group behind StruQ, SecAlign, and Meta-SecAlign, the main line of work on model-level defenses in this area.
- Source of the full text: data/SecPO_paper.pdf in the official repository (Sizhe-Chen/SecPO). A co-author's separate repository also contains a PDF, but it is an earlier version with different affiliations and some different tables, so this summary follows the official repository version, which matches the abstract in the mailing.
- Not a paper from a Chinese institution.

# Simple Features Beat Most AI Control Monitors
> Accepted at a NeurIPS 2026 workshop (Self-Evolving Diversity-Driven Search for Robust AI Systems, EvoRobust), public version on arXiv cs.CR 2610.41713. University of Virginia, University of California San Diego
> 2026

AI control research guards against a capable model quietly sabotaging its work by having a weaker but trusted model monitor its output. Such monitors are usually evaluated on how well they separate honest code from backdoored code, and the standard dataset for this is the APPS backdoor dataset. This paper shows that the dataset can be solved without finding any backdoor. People wrote the honest solutions and language models wrote the backdoors, so coding style alone gives the class away, and problems that are easy to backdoor differ in their metadata, for example by having fewer tests, so the labels leak through the problem as well.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41713 (full text read from paper/main.pdf in the authors' repository, https://github.com/ShubsDS/authorship-not-sabotage)

### Contribution
- Shows that a logistic regression on word frequencies (TF-IDF), which neither runs nor understands the code, reaches an AUROC of 0.806 on the standard test split, beating 20 of the 24 published open-weight monitors. The 95th percentile with shuffled labels is 0.541.
- Shows that a classifier using only three facts about the problem (difficulty, number of tests, statement length) and no code at all reaches 0.766 and also beats 20 monitors. Backdoor-class problems have 20.95 tests on average, about half of the honest class's 38.85.
- Designs a class-swap experiment with Claude Sonnet 5 that directly shows the shortcut reads authorship: pairing human honest code with Sonnet 5's backdoors raises the classifier's score from 0.789 on the original pairs to 0.953.
- Finds that of the 172 backdoors in the standard test split, 17 only trigger on inputs the problem's constraints rule out and 7 already give a wrong answer on a public test, so they are not valid backdoors.
- Proposes three checks that monitor benchmarks can run on themselves (report no-code baselines alongside every monitor score, pair each class with another author's code, and validate trigger inputs) and releases the rebuilt data and scripts under the MIT license.

### Evaluation
- Data: the APPS backdoor dataset (honest solutions by competitive programmers, backdoors by eight models released by early 2025, including Claude 3.7 Sonnet and o3-mini). The standard split has 1,209 training and 1,375 test problems.
- Baselines: the 24 open-weight monitors (0.4B to 32B parameters) from prior work. The four that beat the baseline are all 24B or larger.
- Comparing attackers: on the 99 problems with a working backdoor, at a threshold that flags 2% of honest code, a Sonnet 5 monitor catches 77% of the older models' backdoors and 37% of Sonnet 5's, a gap of 0.40. Keeping only the 54 problems where both backdoors are verified to be real, the numbers become 78% and 61%, a gap of 0.17 whose confidence interval barely excludes zero (0.0002 to 0.32).
- Reliability of the metrics: AUROCs are five-fold cross-validated, confidence intervals come from bootstrapping over problems, and every baseline is reported next to its score on 1,000 label shuffles, which rules out chance. The attacker comparison, however, rests on 54 to 99 problems and a single attacking model.

#### Comment
- The paper does not show that published monitors actually rely on these shortcuts; the authors only argue that a high score on this dataset is not, by itself, evidence of sabotage detection. Even so, it puts concrete numbers on a structural flaw in a benchmark that is widely used in AI control research. An appendix result showing that character count alone separates the classes in four of five agentic ControlArena settings (up to 0.802) suggests the problem is not limited to this one dataset.
- Limitations: the authors ran only two zero-shot monitors, generated backdoors from a single prompt, used Claude models to write the input validators, and searched only a short range of nearby legal inputs.
- Venue: a NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper. Workshop review is lighter than the main conference and the main body is five pages, so the conclusions should be generalized with care.
- Source of the full text: paper/main.pdf in the v1.0 release ("Camera ready: EvoRobust") of the authors' repository. Parts of the repository README still carry numbers from earlier experiments, so this summary follows the figures in the PDF, which match the abstract in the mailing.
- Not a paper from a Chinese institution.
