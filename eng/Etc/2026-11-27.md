# Small Leaks Sink Great Ships: Automated Analysis of Protocols That Tolerate Low-Entropy Keys
> Accepted at NDSS 2027 (DOI 10.14722/ndss.2027.230474), posted on arXiv. Wuhan University, Shandong University
> 2026

WPA2-PSK, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), and Bluetooth Classic (BC) tolerate low-entropy keys for usability, backward compatibility, or resource constraints. Symbolic protocol verifiers such as ProVerif and TAMARIN, however, assume perfect encryption and cannot reason about brute-force attackers, so earlier work has hand-written protocol-specific rules that reveal particular keys to the attacker. The authors propose an automated, protocol-agnostic symbolic method built on three ideas: a message-derivation fact, MDerive, that captures brute-force prerequisites independently of function symbols; a three-tier breaking oracle (value-breaking, relationship-breaking, message-derivation); and an attacker model that caps, in bits, the key length the attacker may exhaust. They implement it as EntropyVerif, a syntax extension of TAMARIN that needs no kernel changes, and use it to find a new protocol-level attack on Bluetooth Classic.

Links:
- arXiv abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33595
- arXiv HTML full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.33595
- arXiv PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33595
- NDSS 2027 DOI: https://dx.doi.org/10.14722/ndss.2027.230474
- Author repository (EntropyVerif): https://github.com/luojiazhishu/entropy-verif

### Contribution
- A protocol-agnostic methodology for low-entropy keys that relaxes the perfect-encryption assumption and supports recursive decomposition of compound keys.
- EntropyVerif, which turns a simple `:= L` annotation on low-entropy terms into standard multiset-rewriting rules, so every proof is still carried out by an unmodified TAMARIN.
- Automatic rediscovery of the PMKID attack (WPA2-PSK) and the KNOB and Method Confusion attacks (BLE pairing) without protocol-specific rules.
- A new attack on Bluetooth Classic, the Unilateral Multi-Downgrade (UMD) attack, which repeatedly downgrades one side's session key length and recombines the recovered fragments. Against devices using the maximum 128-bit key it cuts recovery cost from 2^128 to n·2^(128/n), down to 2^12 for n = 16.

### Evaluation
- Targets: WPA2-PSK, BLE pairing, BC session establishment, and six BC device configurations (combinations of accepted key lengths, such as Secure-Connections-only or legacy-only). TAMARIN ran as the backend on a server with an AMD 7950X3D and 192 GB of RAM.
- Results: compared with models from the literature, the WPA2-PSK model finds KRACK as before and also the PMKID attack, which the original analysis did not reveal; the BLE model finds both KNOB and Method Confusion. For BC, the session key is always exposed whenever the peripheral's minimum accepted key length is within the attacker's capacity, and legacy-compatible devices can be repeatedly downgraded until even the longest session key is recovered.
- Cost: on three Alipay payment-protocol models with no reachable attack, verdicts and proof step counts were unchanged, and run time grew by about 12 seconds (under 9%) to about 4 minutes. The heaviest BC configuration, by contrast, took roughly 9.9×10^4 seconds, and the BC and BLE models depend on hand-written TAMARIN tactics.
- Credibility: the authors state that all analysis happened inside the symbolic model and that no real Bluetooth devices were used to discover or validate UMD. They report disclosing it to the Bluetooth SIG in July 2024, and that in February 2025 the SIG replied that its internal analysis considers the scenario practical.

#### Comment
- Because this comes from Chinese institutions, I checked further. The venue is NDSS 2027, a top-tier security conference, and the NDSS DOI is printed in the paper. I confirmed that the tool repository (luojiazhishu/entropy-verif) exists, and since the results are symbolic analyses reproducible from published TAMARIN models, there is little room for inflated metrics. The comparison targets, existing literature models of the same protocols, are appropriate.
- UMD is not a bug in one product but a design weakness in how the BC specification allows key-length renegotiation, which means it may linger in legacy-compatible Bluetooth devices (cars, audio equipment, and the like). There is no real-device demonstration, so practical difficulty should be judged separately.

# Make the Adversary Spend More: Stateful Deception as a Cost-Exchange Primitive for AI-Era Cyber Defense
> To appear at the 5th Workshop on Active Defense and Deception (AD&D, co-located with ESORICS 2026), posted on arXiv (authors' version before peer review). CNIT, University of Bologna, University of North Carolina Wilmington, University of Parma
> 2026

LLMs lower the attacker's cost of scanning and adapting while tempting defenders into unbounded compute. The authors argue that cyber deception should be judged by the cost exchange it imposes between attacker and defender, not by realism or detection rate, and that the primitive making that exchange favorable is persistent, adversary-specific state. Instead of a honeypot that resets each session or mixes sessions from different attackers, each recognized adversary re-enters the environment it believes it has compromised, kept consistent with its own past actions, while the LLM only renders explicit state and never stores it. They formalize the trade-off as the Deception Exchange Rate (DER), the ratio of mission-weighted adversary effort burned to defender resources spent, and implement it in an SSH honeypot that pairs Cowrie with a local LLM.

Links:
- arXiv abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34770
- arXiv HTML full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34770
- arXiv PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34770

### Contribution
- A position that the unit of AI-era deception is explicit per-adversary state (filesystem, tools, credentials, services, interaction history), not the honeypot instance.
- DER, a comparative metric with explicit terms for shared infrastructure, per-adversary state, AI inference, energy, human oversight, and mission risk.
- Measurements of the DER denominator (defender cost) on a running generative honeypot, and two failure modes: a gap between benchmark and production quality, and chain-of-thought leakage from reasoning models.

### Evaluation
- Setup: five open-weight models (Llama 3.1 8B, Gemma 2 9B-IT, Gemma 4 E4B, Gemma 4 26B-A4B, Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B) as response generators in a Cowrie-based SSH honeypot.
- Cost: a deterministic layer (Cowrie's native commands), a response cache, prompt compression, and related optimizations cut tokens per session by about 63% (from roughly 13,134 to 4,855), model calls from 10 to 4, and mean latency from about 3 s to about 1.5 s.
- Quality: Gemma 4 26B-A4B scored about 97% good responses on the benchmark but only about 57% in production before mitigation, while Llama 3.1 8B rose from about 95% to about 100%. Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B reasoned aloud on almost every command, making its output unusable as shell responses. After mitigation, Gemma 4 26B-A4B recovered to about 100% on a 13-command production suite.
- Credibility: as the authors state, this paper measures only the defender side; per-adversary state and the DER numerator (adversary effort burned) are specified but not measured. The production suite is also small, at 13 commands.

#### Comment
- From an MTD and deception standpoint, this is closer to a position paper that recasts the known weakness of LLM honeypots, losing state consistency, as a cost metric. The practical observation that reasoning models give the honeypot away by leaking their chain of thought is immediately useful.
- As a workshop paper it has lighter review than a main conference, and its central claim about the effect on attackers is not yet backed by data. No code link was found in the paper.

# Northstar: Efficient Verifiable Negacyclic Polynomial Arithmetic with Applications to Encrypted Lattice-based Multi-Asset ERC-20 and Verifiable FHE
> Extended version of a paper accepted at Financial Cryptography and Data Security 2027 (FC 2027), posted on arXiv. Affiliation unconfirmed. Abstract-only summary, full text not read
> 2026

Lattice-based cryptography underpins a wide range of post-quantum schemes, from signatures and encryption to fully homomorphic encryption (FHE), but verifying that lattice computations were done correctly is still expensive, especially for compute-heavy FHE operations and in constrained settings such as on-chain verification by smart contracts. The authors say they propose Northstar, a technique for proving negacyclic polynomial arithmetic natively.

Links:
- arXiv abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34417
- arXiv HTML full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34417
- arXiv PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34417

### Contribution
- According to the authors, a technique that plugs into existing STARK proving frameworks to prove negacyclic arithmetic efficiently.
- The authors claim Northstar enables the first verifiable encrypted multi-asset (about 1,000 assets) variant of ERC-20 that a smart contract can verify efficiently and that is practically deployable on today's blockchains.

### Evaluation
- According to the abstract, Northstar achieves the best overall performance across prover time, verifier time, and proof size compared with prior state-of-the-art approaches for verifiable FHE. The abstract gives no specific numbers.
- Because the full text was not read, the datasets, comparison conditions, and credibility of the metrics could not be checked.
