# Rented Oscillations: Multi-Tenant Data Center Workload Synchronization Attacks and Mitigation
> arXiv preprint (not peer reviewed). No affiliation given in the paper (last author is at KAUST, Saudi Arabia)
> 2026

Multi-tenant data centers that rent out workstations on demand are usually assumed to draw steady power, because inference, CPU jobs and training average each other out. The authors describe an adversary who uses forged identities or stolen payment details to open many accounts, rents GPUs in one region, and runs deliberately unoptimized LLM training whose compute and idle phases form square-wave power profiles, then aligns them. Each job looks like ordinary, if inefficient, training, but together they inject MW-scale forced oscillations into the sub-10 Hz band where the transmission grid's electromechanical dynamics live. The paper builds a facility model from measured power traces, assesses grid impact with small-signal, large-signal and Floquet analysis, and proposes detection that attributes oscillations to workloads by their signal-energy contribution, plus mitigation that re-times workloads so their oscillations cancel.

### Contribution
- Measures attacker capability on hardware rather than assuming it: driving an RTX PRO 4500 Blackwell GPU at 20-80% duty ratios and 0.2-10 Hz shows it tracks a square wave faithfully only up to about 5 Hz, which bounds the threat model.
- Shows oscillations can arise without any adversary: when a few large training jobs share similar or harmonically related iteration rates, they never dephase, and an uncoordinated facility still swings by 29%.
- Uses Floquet analysis to show that MW-scale forcing saturates the limiters of generators' power system stabilizers (PSS) and automatic voltage regulators (AVR), amplifying its own effect and creating a nearly undamped mode near the forcing frequency.
- Proposes detection based on each workload's (optionally band-limited) signal-energy contribution, and mitigation that slows workloads to a common base frequency and then optimizes their delays by coordinate descent to minimize oscillatory energy, extended to geographically distributed data centers.

### Evaluation
- CPU nodes on a top-20 supercomputer modulated at 1 Hz produced a 0.2 V peak-to-peak voltage oscillation observed at the authors' lab about 1 km away on the same substation.
- On a modified IEEE 68-bus system with a 420 MW data center, the large-signal response peaked at 2.06 Hz at G7, about 19 dB above the small-signal prediction, and Floquet analysis found a mode at the forcing frequency with about 0.5% damping.
- With the facility split into 5,040 nodes of 50 kW, detection ranked all 3,455 nodes of the three large jobs above every background node, with no misattribution. For 100 workloads synchronized at 1 Hz, mitigation cut RMS oscillation from 30.7 MW to 4.6 MW, computed in 1.2 s.
- In a power hardware-in-the-loop (PHIL) experiment on a surrogate Saudi transmission model (799 buses, 73 generators), 20 compute nodes scaled to a 1 GW facility reproduced the attack; detection fired within the first cycle and mitigation cut demand RMS oscillation from 199 to 31 MW (84%) and generator frequency oscillation from 67 to 20 mHz (70%). With two distributed data centers, voltage oscillation on 47 target buses fell by 47-76% while keeping 96.8% of throughput.
- Reliability: GPU measurements come from a single card, and both the supercomputer and PHIL experiments used CPU nodes replaying model-generated profiles, not GPUs. The grids are benchmark or surrogate models built from public information, with settings that accentuate impact, such as G7 inertia reduced to 70%. Whether a 1 GW-scale attack can actually get past rental quotas and identity checks is argued, not demonstrated.

#### Comment
- Treating cloud rental as a vehicle for load-altering attacks is the distinctive idea: earlier attacks had to compromise large fleets of devices such as air conditioners, whereas here the load is simply bought.
- The mitigation does not need to tell attacks from naturally arising synchronization, which makes it practical, and the authors also suggest operators require tenants to declare training up front and verify compliance. No code or data address is given in the paper.
- The paper states no affiliations. The Saudi grid case study, the lab sharing a substation with a top-20 supercomputer, and a web search confirming that the last author, Charalambos Konstantinou, is a faculty member at KAUST (King Abdullah University of Science and Technology) point to a KAUST team. The other authors' affiliations were not confirmed, and nothing indicates a Chinese institution.
