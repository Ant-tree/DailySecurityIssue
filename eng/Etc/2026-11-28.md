# InstantCoFFEA: Fast and accurate Tor flow correlation at realistic scale
> arXiv preprint (not peer-reviewed). Ewha Womans University, The University of Texas at El Paso (UTEP)
> 2026

End-to-end correlation attacks on Tor link a client to its destination by matching packet timing and size seen at both ends of a circuit, at the entry guard and at the exit. Deep learning methods such as DeepCorr and DeepCoFFEA made these attacks far more accurate, yet the real-world threat stayed unclear. The correlation stage compares every egress-ingress pair, an O(N²) cost that becomes unmanageable at scale, and past evaluations did not reflect how many flows a real adversary sees or how rare true matches are (the base rate). The authors propose InstantCoFFEA, which replaces exhaustive comparison with approximate nearest neighbor search, and use the Tor path simulator TorPS to estimate realistic flow counts and base rates before re-measuring attack performance under those conditions.

### Contribution
- Breaks down DeepCoFFEA's runtime and shows the bottleneck is not embedding but sorting and voting over the N² similarity matrix; at N of about 200k, sorting and voting account for 96.0% of runtime excluding embedding. For a fair comparison, DeepCoFFEA's sorting and voting were also moved to the GPU.
- Indexes ingress embeddings with CAGRA, a GPU graph-based approximate nearest neighbor index, retrieves only the top k candidates (k=32) for each egress window, and applies ranked voting among them. No full sort is needed, and voting is limited to the candidates.
- Updates TorPS to Tor 0.4.9 path selection (family identifiers, the current guard algorithm, IPv6 /32 exclusion) and combines the May 2026 consensus, the Tranco list and RouteViews and CAIDA data to estimate the flow counts and base rates that relay-level and network-level (AS and IXP) adversaries would actually face.

### Evaluation
- Dataset: the roughly 60,000 website flow pairs released by DeepCoFFEA. Runtime at the 200k scale was measured on duplicated flows with added timing jitter, which the appendix shows take about 93.5% of the time real flows do.
- Speed: excluding embedding, correlation at N=199,991 took 572.94 s for DeepCoFFEA and 39.35 s for InstantCoFFEA. Per flow pair, InstantCoFFEA was 6.89 times faster at N=100,058 and 14.56 times faster at N=199,991.
- Accuracy (lab setting): against DeepCoFFEA, DeepCorr and RAPTOR, approximate search cost no accuracy, and PR-AUC at the 200k scale rose from 0.94 to 0.96 (five random splits).
- Realistic setting: a relay adversary with 2,000 MB/s of bandwidth (1.05% of the network) sees 13,889 guard-side and 36,300 exit-side flows but only 352 true matches, a base rate of 6.98×10⁻⁵%. PR-AUC dropped for every method here, with InstantCoFFEA at 0.53 and DeepCoFFEA at 0.42. At larger network-adversary scales, DeepCoFFEA ran out of 48 GB of GPU memory building the full similarity matrix, while InstantCoFFEA still ran.
- Reliability: prior methods were rerun on the same hardware using a public dataset and a public simulator. The authors note that TorPS models circuits rather than traffic, so each circuit was treated as one flow, and since a circuit can carry several streams the real flow counts may be higher. Conflux, Tor 0.4.8's multipath feature, was not modeled. No code link was found in the paper.

#### Comment
- The conclusion leans on evaluation methodology more than attack progress: in the authors' words, computation is no longer the main obstacle to large-scale correlation, the low base rate is. It quantifies why defenses against correlation should also be evaluated at realistic base rates.
- Since this makes an attack more efficient, the worrying point for Tor users is that correlation still reaches a PR-AUC of about 0.5 even at a very low base rate.
