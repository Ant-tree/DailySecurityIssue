# Beyond a Single Commit: Anchor-Guided Reconstruction of Multi-Commit Patches
> arXiv cs.CR 2611.09026 (preprint, not peer reviewed). University of Wollongong (Australia), University of International Relations (China), Tsinghua University (China), Purdue University (USA)
> 2026

Linking a vulnerability to the commits that fix it underpins vulnerability analysis, remediation and security tooling. In practice, though, fixes often span more than one commit: the same flaw may appear in several places and need similar fixes, or a first fix may be incomplete and get patched up later. The paper illustrates this with a TensorFlow vulnerability first addressed by a March 2018 commit and later completed by additional commits. Existing tools look at commits in isolation and miss these multi-commit patches.

The authors propose HARPOON. It first picks anchor commits, the ones most likely to fix the target vulnerability. It then fills in missing locations by finding commits with remediation patterns similar to the anchors (coverage completion), and reconstructs the fixing process by following commits with direct or indirect dependencies on the anchors (dependency reconstruction). Finally, an LLM checks each candidate's intent and discards commits that look similar or are dependent but serve a different purpose.

Links:
- arXiv abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09026
- arXiv HTML full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09026
- arXiv PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09026

### Contribution
- A patch identification framework that handles multi-commit patches explicitly. Because it starts from anchor commits and narrows the search instead of feeding every commit to an LLM, it avoids the error build-up LLMs suffer on long commit histories.
- A new benchmark with commit-level multi-commit annotations. From 30 popular projects (ten each in C/C++, Java and Python), the authors collected 2,374 CVEs with public fixing commits and built an evaluation set of 1,207 vulnerabilities (1,019 single-commit and 188 multi-commit).
- In the wild, HARPOON found 895 patches missing from public vulnerability databases, 663 of them previously unrecorded fixes for 323 CRITICAL- or HIGH-severity vulnerabilities.

### Evaluation
- Baselines: four state-of-the-art tools, TAPER, VFCFinder, SHIP and Prospector. Because their definitions differ, the comparison is split three ways to measure commit-level accuracy and coverage separately.
- Results: F1 of 93.02% on single-commit patches and 75.08% on multi-commit patches, well ahead of the baselines. GPT-5-Nano is the default LLM for its cost-effectiveness; the paper also includes an ablation and a cost analysis.
- Reliability of the metrics: the authors built the ground-truth annotations themselves from NVD, Debian Security, the GitHub Advisory Database and manual review, so the label definitions may favor the proposed method. No code or dataset link was found in the paper.

#### Comment
- Given how many CVEs lack patch information, finding hundreds of missing patches in practice is valuable. The work bears directly on patch-data quality for supply-chain vulnerability management and SCA tools.
- Chinese-institution check: the first author and the corresponding authors (Siqi Ma, Willy Susilo) are at the University of Wollongong; the Chinese co-authors are Xu Gai (University of International Relations) and Chao Zhang (Tsinghua University), and Elisa Bertino (Purdue) also contributes. All are real researchers and institutions with an established security track record. The lead institution is Australian, and the results openly show the method's weaker performance on the harder multi-commit case, so the numbers do not look inflated. However, neither code nor benchmark has been released, so reproducibility cannot be confirmed for now.

# Empirical Study of Post-Quantum Cryptography Adoption Status in SSH Infrastructure
> arXiv cs.CR 2611.09703 (preprint, not peer reviewed). Bitsight Technologies (USA)
> 2026

To prepare for "Q-day", when quantum computers could break today's public-key cryptography, major CDNs and cloud providers are already moving to post-quantum cryptography (PQC). Prior work mostly benchmarks PQC performance; how widely it is actually deployed is far less clear. Using proprietary internet-scan data from the security ratings firm Bitsight, this paper analyzes the SSH key-exchange algorithms offered by 1.52 million hosts on port 22, owned by 27,012 organizations.

Links:
- arXiv abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09703
- arXiv HTML full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09703
- arXiv PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09703

### Contribution
- A large-scale snapshot of real PQC deployment in SSH. At the host level, 45.2% support a PQC key exchange and 54.8% are classical-only. At the organization level, 39.9% have PQC on at least one host and 60.1% have none.
- A breakdown of which PQC algorithms are in use: sntrup761x25519-sha512@openssh.com leads at 44.86%, its standardized name sntrup761x25519-sha512 appears on 11.08%, and mlkem768x25519-sha256 on 5.79% (servers advertise several algorithms at once, so the shares overlap). Rare variants such as mlkem1024nistp384, frodokem and kyber1024 hybrids also show up.
- Correlates PQC adoption with industry, geography and organizational size (digital footprint and financial size).

### Evaluation
- The data are SSH fingerprints gathered by a commercial scanner; hosts are attributed to organizations with Bitsight's AI-based asset attribution engine.
- Reliability of the metrics: in SSH, the OpenSSH version's defaults often decide PQC support, so it is hard to tell deliberate migration from the side effect of routine upgrades. The data and attribution engine are proprietary, so outsiders cannot reproduce the results, and only port 22 is covered.

#### Comment
- More an industry status report than a research contribution, but a useful baseline for anyone shaping PQC migration policy. Keep in mind that PQC in SSH here concerns key exchange only; host-key signatures are still classical.
- A companion to the TLS paper below, posted the same day by the same authors.

# Empirical Assessment of Post-Quantum Cryptography Adoption Status in TLS
> arXiv cs.CR 2611.09707 (preprint, not peer reviewed). Bitsight Technologies (USA)
> 2026

The same team measures PQC deployment across seven services that run over TLS: HTTPS (443), LDAPS (636), SMTPS (465), IMAP-SSL (993), POP3-SSL (995), TELNETS (992) and RDP (3389). Since PQC key exchange is only available in TLS 1.3, they sampled 10,000 IP addresses per service and checked whether the hybrid key exchange X25519MLKEM768 is negotiated during the TLS handshake.

Links:
- arXiv abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09707
- arXiv HTML full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09707
- arXiv PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09707

### Contribution
- The first side-by-side comparison of PQC adoption that goes beyond the web. At the origin-server level, PQC support is 9.1% for HTTPS and 7.39% for LDAPS, and below 1% for the other five services. At the organization level, it is 11% for HTTPS, 5.4% for LDAPS, 4% for IMAP-SSL, 3% for POP3-SSL and under 2% for SMTPS, TELNETS and RDP.
- A separate measurement of Cloudflare-hosted sites: with the server name (SNI) set, about 83.6% of 11,979 domain scans negotiate X25519MLKEM768, but connecting to 11,048 Cloudflare IPs without SNI finds PQC on only about 4%. The gap shows how much the measurement method changes the answer.
- Correlation with organization size and industry suggests that large enterprises start early thanks to budgets and staff, but the sheer size of their networks makes a complete rollout harder.

### Evaluation
- The sample of 10,000 IPs per service is small, and the results can shift with the sampling method. Handshakes were tested with openssl, and organizations were attributed with the same proprietary engine as in the SSH paper.
- Reliability of the metrics: as the Cloudflare experiment shows, IP-level and domain-level measurements diverge sharply, so "9.1% for HTTPS" should not be read as the web-wide adoption rate. It is also measured in different units from Cloudflare's own claim that over half of human traffic is protected by PQC.

#### Comment
- The most practically useful finding is that PQC has barely started in protocols closer to internal enterprise infrastructure, such as mail, remote access and directory services.
- Like the SSH paper, this is an industry report built on commercial data and is hard to reproduce externally.
