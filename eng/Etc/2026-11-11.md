# Reconfiguring System-Level Protection Through Bounded Delegation in RISC-V MCUs
> arXiv preprint (not peer reviewed)
> 2026

Embedded systems built on microcontrollers (MCUs) increasingly need to hand memory regions or accelerators from one domain to another at runtime, for example during an over-the-air (OTA) update. Commercial MCU protection mechanisms restrict who may change protection rules, but not what rules an authorized domain may create. A domain that has been delegated configuration authority can therefore widen a rule beyond its intended region or create rules that overlap another domain's boundaries. This paper names bounded configuration authority as the missing primitive and proposes an ownership-aware model: every rule has an owner, and before a change is committed the hardware checks that a delegated rule stays inside its owner's enclosing rule. The model is implemented in the open-source RISC-V World Checker and integrated into a CVA6-based SoC.

Links:
- arXiv abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12622
- arXiv HTML full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12622
- arXiv PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12622

### Contribution
- Surveys existing MCU protection mechanisms and shows that controlling access to the configuration interface does not bound policy authority.
- Proposes a model in which explicit ownership and rule containment limit both configuration authority and recursive delegation. Checks happen only on the configuration path, so the normal access-checking datapath is not lengthened.
- Lets a delegated domain update policy directly, without going through firmware or a hypervisor, which greatly cuts policy-transition latency.

### Evaluation
- Hardware cost, access latency, reconfiguration latency, and security enforcement were evaluated on a single-hart CVA6 SoC on a Xilinx Genesys2 FPGA.
- With eight rules, the design added 7.1% LUTs and 1.2% FFs (from 6,157 to 6,595 LUTs) with no loss in access latency or operating frequency. Rule counts from 1 to 64 were measured.
- In an OTA update case study, direct reconfiguration by the delegated domain reduced policy-transition latency by 89.5% compared with hypervisor mediation and by 94.6% compared with trusted firmware (OpenSBI) mediation.
- Scenarios confirmed that legitimate refinement is allowed while changes that exceed the owner's range or overlap another domain are rejected before commit.
- Identifier forgery, checker bypass, physical attacks, and denial of service are outside the threat model.

#### Comment
- A collaboration between the University of Minho in Portugal (Sandro Pinto's group) and Infineon Technologies; the many industry co-authors tie it closely to real MCU design. The authors aim to feed the results into RISC-V International's specification work.
- The authors promise to open-source all artifacts, but no link is in the paper yet.
- Evaluation is on a single-hart FPGA prototype, so area and power costs on production MCUs still need confirmation.
- Full text source: arXiv PDF (downloaded on the user's computer).

# Public, Identifiable, and Effectively Immutable: Ethical Lessons from Research on arXiv
> Accepted at the 19th Workshop on Cyber Security Experimentation and Test (CSET 26, co-located with ACSAC 26), arXiv preprint. RWTH Aachen University (Germany), University of Southern Denmark (Denmark), Florida International University (USA)
> 2026

When authors post a paper, arXiv also takes the LaTeX source files and distributes them publicly, which many authors do not realize. Leftover comments, to-do lists, text the author thought was deleted, and even credentials or private keys can end up exposed, and arXiv does not allow such accidental disclosures to be corrected. Three recent studies (one by the authors) analyzed these source files; all three included ethics statements, yet they reached different conclusions on questions such as whether to release their tooling and whether following embedded links is acceptable. Drawing on discussions with researchers, affected authors, and readers, this experience paper maps the ethical setting of arXiv as a dataset that is public, identifiable by real names, and effectively immutable, and distills four dilemmas and four lessons.

- Authors' group PDF: https://www.comsys.rwth-aachen.de/publication/2026/2026_pennekamp_public-identifiable-effectively-immutable/2026_pennekamp_public-identifiable-effectively-immutable.pdf

### Contribution
- Splits the data into dimensions by whether it is self-contained (comments, profanity, obsolete text, which are effectively unfixable) or externally actionable (access tokens, credentials, private keys, which are partly fixable), and lays out the remediability and sensitivity of each.
- Derives four dilemmas: quantifying sensitive information requires reading it; validation attempts versus over-reporting; problem scale versus available resources (human attention, and compute when LLMs are used); and the awareness benefit of notification versus the extra attention it draws to the exposed information.
- Offers four lessons: public availability is the starting point of ethical reasoning, not its conclusion; research should minimize how much it amplifies exposed information; disclosure and notification should be calibrated to harm, confidence, and whether recipients can actually act; and effective immutability must be considered throughout collection, validation, and disclosure.

### Evaluation
- This is an experience paper with no quantitative experiments. The argument is built by comparing the ethical decisions of three earlier studies of arXiv source files (600k submissions, the full corpus, and 100k submissions analyzed with LLMs), such as not testing credentials, withholding analysis code, and not running responsible disclosure.
- Reliability: the evidence rests on the authors' experience, discussions, and earlier ethics statements, which limits generalization. One author also wrote one of the studies being compared.

#### Comment
- Credibility: accepted at CSET 26, a peer-reviewed workshop co-located with ACSAC 26. The paper lists RWTH Aachen COMSYS, the University of Southern Denmark, and Florida International University as affiliations.
- Practical guidance for any security research that uses public repositories (arXiv, Zenodo, the Wayback Machine, and so on) as data. Repository-side fixes, such as no longer serving source files, are deliberately left out in favor of researchers' responsibilities.
