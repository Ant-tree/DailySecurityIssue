# "I Just Press Allow": Understanding Privacy Practices of New Internet Users in Urban India
> PoPETs 2026 Issue 2 (Privacy Enhancing Technologies Symposium), journal-style conference
> 2026

In rapidly digitizing countries such as India, many people come online for the first time through smartphones. With little prior exposure to digital systems, they meet apps and services that demand personal data while leaving them little real choice. This paper is a qualitative study of 50 first-time smartphone users across diverse geographic and socio-economic backgrounds in India, examining how they understand and act on privacy risks.

Paper: https://petsymposium.org/popets/2026/popets-2026-0043.pdf

### Contribution
- Extends privacy research, usually centered on experienced users, to new and low-literacy users.
- Reveals where social contexts (phones shared within families, taken to repair shops, set up by acquaintances) overlap with privacy risk.

### Evaluation
- A qualitative study based on interviews with 50 first-time smartphone users; the method is verifiable from the open PDF and GitHub materials.
- As a qualitative study, it focuses on understanding context and patterns rather than statistical generalization.

#### Comment
- The point that technical mobile-security defenses may not reach low-literacy or new users is a useful reference for designing mobile-security products aimed at global markets. It is filed under "Other" as it fits none of the three categories.
- Joint work by BITS Pilani (Hyderabad) and the University of Sydney, with an open PDF, published in PoPETs.
