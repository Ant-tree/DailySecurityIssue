# MobileProbe: Adaptive Security Evaluation of Mobile Agents via Trajectory-Aware Injection
> Accepted at NDSS 2027 (peer reviewed, DOI 10.14722/ndss.2027.230546). arXiv version. The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, Zhejiang University, Huawei
> 2026 (conference in 2027)

Mobile GUI agents read the phone screen and operate apps for the user, which exposes them to environmental injection: instructions hidden in whatever the screen shows, such as emails, messages or in-app dialogs, can redirect what the agent does. Existing benchmarks pin hand-written payloads to a fixed step of a fixed task. Those payloads do not carry over to other apps, and an agent that takes a different route and never reaches the injected screen is recorded as "safe" without ever being tested. MobileProbe is a red-teaming tool that picks the injection point and the payload based on the path the agent actually takes. It first runs automated pentesting to find screen content an attacker can control and stores it as templates, injects only when the agent reaches a matching page, and refines candidate payloads with an evolutionary loop that uses a surrogate model as a filter. The attack channels are limited to two that an unprivileged third party can use: malicious text inside ordinary content, and a fake dialog shown by a repackaged app in its own window.

### Contribution
- Identifies two weaknesses of fixed-point benchmarks (attack instances tied to one app and task, and agents on other paths never being exposed) and formulates injection-point selection and payload generation as a joint search conditioned on the agent's realized trajectory.
- Builds 83 attack instances across 12 apps on top of MobileWorld tasks (44 malicious text, 39 fake dialog, 47 cross-app), each labelled with a confidentiality, integrity or availability goal and paired with a program that checks whether the attack succeeded.
- Explains why other channels (overlay windows, which need SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW, system notifications, icon spoofing) were left out, keeping the threat model to unprivileged attackers.

### Evaluation
- Target agents: Gemini-3.1-Pro (a commercial model that takes raw screenshots), an agent framework that adds a UI grounding module to Gemini, and the open on-device model GUI-Owl-1.5-8B.
- Payload miss rate (the agent never sees the payload): 13.3%, 12.0% and 22.9% with expert-chosen fixed points, versus 4.8%, 3.6% and 6.0% with MobileProbe.
- Attack success rate: 85.1%, 86.3% and 85.1% for MobileProbe. With the same injection points, templates from existing benchmarks reached 6.8-34.5%, one-shot LLM generation 8.0-15.7%, PAIR ported to mobile 54.6-77.5%, and Genesis 39.0-61.4%. Fake dialogs (88.9-95.7%) worked better than malicious text (78.0-81.8%), and availability attacks were the hardest (75.0-81.0%).
- The share of successful attacks where the original task still finished (how unnoticeable the attack is) was 68.1% on Gemini and 46.4% on GUI-Owl. Removing evolutionary refinement dropped success to 28.5%. Ten annotators rated 20 payloads by eye (authenticity 4.3-4.7, behavioral plausibility 3.2-3.4 on a 5-point scale).
- Reliability: PAIR and Genesis were built for web or text settings, and the authors ported them to mobile themselves, so part of the gap may come from the port. Success is judged by per-instance programs rather than an LLM judge, which is more objective.

#### Comment
- Because the authors are at Chinese institutions (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, Zhejiang University, Huawei), we checked further. The venue is NDSS 2027: the DOI in the paper matches the acceptance notice on the lab site of corresponding author Haibo Hu, and Haibo Hu and Qingqing Ye are real faculty members at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University. We found no code or dataset link in the paper, so reproducibility could not be confirmed. The success rates are far above the baselines, but the paper also explains its failures (the same agent can take different paths across runs) and its limitations, so it does not read as overstated.
- Lightweight on-device models (GUI-Owl) were easier to fool under every method, which matters for anyone shipping agents that run on the phone itself.
- Finance and healthcare apps are not in the benchmark.
- Full text source: arXiv PDF (downloaded on the user's computer).
