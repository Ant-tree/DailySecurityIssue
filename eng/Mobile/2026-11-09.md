# Consensus-Constrained Dual-Federated Optimization with Client-Side Dynamic Weighting for Distributed Android Device Threat Defense
> arXiv cs.LG 2611.09470 (cross-listed to cs.CR; preprint, not peer reviewed). University of Tehran (Iran), Sejong University (South Korea)
> 2026

Detecting Android malware on phones, smart-home hubs and wearables benefits from data across many devices, but pooling raw data raises privacy and scalability concerns. Federated learning lets devices train together without sharing data, yet partial participation and uneven local model quality can destabilize global optimization. This paper proposes DW-FedADMM, which adds performance-dependent client weighting to FedADMM, a primal-dual coordination method. The dual variables regulate disagreement between local and global models, and the dynamic coefficient adjusts each client's local state before the augmented-Lagrangian update.

### Contribution
- Combines consensus-constrained primal-dual federated optimization with performance-dependent dynamic weighting.
- Compares against five federated baselines, including variants that apply the same weighting to FedAvg and FedProx (DW-FedAvg, DW-FedProx), all on one shared code base.

### Evaluation
- Data: the released preprocessed feature representations of three public Android malware benchmarks, Drebin and Malgenome (215 features each) and TUANDROMD (4,465 apps, 241 features).
- Setup: the same four-layer feed-forward network (hidden layers of 200, 100 and 50 units) for every method; an 80:20 split with training data divided evenly (IID) across 5, 10 or 15 clients; partial participation; budgets of 5 and 10 communication rounds; ten random seeds per configuration.
- Results: the best seed-averaged accuracy on Drebin (97.64% ± 0.20%) and the best mean accuracy in 9 of 18 configurations (7 of 9 with a 10-round budget). Pooled over all configurations: 95.42% accuracy, 94.62% F1, 96.06% precision and 99.45% AUC, slightly above FedADMM (95.32%, 94.51%, 95.56%, 99.41%), with the lowest mean false-positive rate.
- Reliability: ten seeds and a shared code base are good practice. However, the gap over FedADMM is around 0.1 percentage points, too small to call a practical advantage, and IID partitioning sidesteps the central difficulty of federated learning, data heterogeneity across devices (the authors list it as next work). Drebin and Malgenome are old datasets that do not represent today's malware landscape.

#### Comment
- Framed as a mobile security application, but in substance a federated optimization paper. On-device training cost, communication volume and malicious clients (model poisoning), the real security issues of mobile federated learning, are not addressed.
- Venue: a preprint by university researchers in Iran and South Korea. Public datasets, a configuration table and the authors' code repository (per a footnote) make reproduction fairly easy.
- Full text source: official arXiv PDF downloaded on the user's computer.
