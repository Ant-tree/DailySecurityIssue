Subcategory: Privacy (dark patterns in vehicle infotainment)

# User Perceptions of Deceptive In-Vehicle Infotainment User Interfaces
> Accepted at NDSS 2027, arXiv preprint. Clemson University, Florida International University
> 2026

Consent decisions about the driver, passenger and vehicle data that cars collect are increasingly made on infotainment screens, yet dark patterns in vehicles have received little attention compared with the web and mobile apps. The authors study Android Automotive OS (AAOS) in three phases. First, 26 HCI and privacy annotators audited AAOS emulators from Honda, Volvo, GM and Polestar and documented 17 dark-pattern types across 65 observations. Next, 100 participants watched screen recordings of how GMC, Honda and Volvo each implement the same dark patterns, so that differences in perception could be traced to implementation choices. Finally, 49 separate participants rated automotive and e-commerce dark-pattern clips before and after a short tutorial. The studies point to a "sophistication paradox": the subtler a dark pattern is, the less suspicion it raises and the harder it is to spot.

### Contribution
- The first systematic dark-pattern audit of real OEM AAOS builds. Applying the existing taxonomies of Gray et al. and Brignull, it finds Preselection (20.0% of observations) and Forced Consent with no way to decline (18.5%) to be the most common. It also shows real screens of Bundling, where turning off data sharing also disables safety services such as Automatic Collision Notification.
- The first experiment showing that the same dark pattern can be perceived differently depending on how each OEM implements it. The practical takeaway is that oversight should look at how a pattern is implemented, not only whether it is present.
- The cross-OEM distribution suggests an industry-wide practice rather than brand-specific design philosophies.

### Evaluation
- Phase 1: 26 annotators inspected four emulators independently. Because each annotator reported different instances, a single inter-rater agreement coefficient could not be computed; instead three authors jointly reviewed every candidate observation.
- Phase 2: 100 adults recruited on Prolific each saw one randomly assigned OEM version of five dark patterns and rated them on eleven seven-point attitude scales. A linear mixed-effects model with a per-participant random intercept showed Honda's Bundling implementation rated worse than GMC's on four attitudes after Bonferroni correction (Control: β = -1.636, p < 0.001). Patterns that are coercive or hidden by design, such as Forced Consent and Sneaking, varied little across OEMs.
- Phase 3: 49 participants rated seven automotive and seven e-commerce clips before and after a tutorial. Before instruction, automotive interfaces were rated higher on ten of eleven attitudes; afterwards the gap largely closed, and on avoidance of unintentional sharing automotive was rated worse.
- Reliability: real OEM emulator screens and explicit statistical corrections are strengths. However, participants rated recordings of emulator screens in an online survey rather than behaving in a real car, and each condition has roughly 30 participants.

#### Comment
- Accepted at NDSS 2027 (held in Seoul), a top-tier security venue.
- It extends mobile dark-pattern research to automotive Android and captures what makes the car different: a driver cannot simply leave the screen or switch to another service.
- Limitation: only the screens shipped in the emulators were audited, which may differ from firmware in production vehicles.

# TRACE-5G: Trust-Aware Runtime Attestation and Continuous Enforcement for 5G User Equipment
> Accepted at IEEE CCNC 2027, arXiv preprint. George Mason University, Idaho National Laboratory
> 2026

5G Authentication and Key Agreement (5G-AKA) authenticates the subscriber credential but says nothing about the integrity of the user equipment (UE) itself. Earlier work added admission-time device certificates and TPM measured-boot attestation, but neither reveals whether unauthorized software runs after the device is admitted. TRACE-5G keeps the pre-admission checks (outer certificate, inner certificate, TPM PCR 0-7 boot attestation), places the admitted UE in restricted connectivity, and continuously verifies its runtime state with Linux IMA and Keylime. Once the UE is trusted, full access is granted on the same PDU session by changing firewall policy only; if trust is lost, access is withdrawn and the network deregisters the UE through the standard procedure.

Links:
- arXiv abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25272
- arXiv HTML full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.25272
- arXiv PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25272

### Contribution
- Organizes UE trust into three levels (Level 0 pre-admission, Level 1 restricted access, Level 2 full access conditioned on continuous attestation) and ties network authorization directly to runtime attestation results.
- Uses a container-aware IMA template (the cgpath field) to apply separate allow-lists for the host and for each container.
- Enforces trust without new NAS/RRC message formats and without DNN changes, IP reassignment or PDU-session re-establishment.

### Evaluation
- Testbed: a 5G SA network with an Open5GS core, a srsRAN gNB on a USRP B210, and a srsUE device with a hardware TPM 2.0 (Nuvoton NPCT75x).
- Across ten trusted trials, Level 2 access was reached about 9-12 s after runtime monitoring started. Across ten untrusted trials the same enforcement sequence was observed; in a representative trace the restrictive policy was applied within 5 ms of the Untrusted verdict and UE-context release completed within 1.670 s.
- Reliability: a small experiment focused on functional validation and latency. The attack scenario is essentially a single container outside the allow-list, and scalability with many UEs or false-positive rates were not evaluated. The pre-admission stage reuses results from the authors' earlier Battlefield 5G work.

#### Comment
- The design targets tactical or private 5G networks where the operator controls device enrollment, so it does not transfer directly to consumer smartphones. Requiring a hardware TPM and a modified kernel on the UE is another constraint.
- IEEE CCNC is an established conference in consumer communications and networking, though its review is not as demanding as top-tier security venues.
