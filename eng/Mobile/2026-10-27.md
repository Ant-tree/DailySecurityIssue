# Label-Budgeted Attribute Inference from Protected Features in Edge-Cloud Split Multimodal LLMs
> arXiv cs.CR (preprint, not peer reviewed), 5-page short paper
> 2026

In edge-cloud split inference, a multimodal LLM is partitioned between a device such as a phone or camera and a server, so the raw image never leaves the device. That is often taken as a privacy win. This paper puts the assumption to the test. It assumes an honest-but-curious server that follows the protocol but tries to infer the age, gender and race of the person in a face image from the intermediate features it legitimately receives. Leakage is measured as a curve: how much of the unprotected leakage the server recovers as its budget of labeled protected features grows from zero to a full set. The findings are that moving the split deeper barely reduces attribute leakage, and that existing protections still leave most of it recoverable with only a handful of labels. Safe-LLaVA, for instance, is trained to refuse biometric questions in its responses, yet from the features it transmits during split inference, 20 labels per class recover 75-85% of the unprotected leakage.

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32079

### Contribution
- Defines attacker strength by a single knob, the label budget of protected samples, and ties together the zero-budget attacker (a classifier trained on unprotected features and applied as is), few-label fine-tuning and full retraining in one recovery curve. Normalizing each score against the unprotected leakage puts metrics as different as balanced accuracy and age error on the same axis.
- Adds a classifier-free dependence test based on normalized HSIC, since a failed probe does not prove the information is gone.
- Compares defenses that intervene at different points, input level (ImageProtector), model level (Safe-LLaVA) and representation level (PASS), within one framework, and shows that their native metrics (refusal rate, fixed-classifier accuracy) do not reflect privacy at the split boundary.

### Evaluation
- Models: LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B. Data: FairFace (9 age groups, gender, 7 race classes) and UTKFace (gender, 5 race classes, age in years). An RTX 2080 Ti stands in for the device and a V100 for the server, with latency, memory and energy reported per split layer.
- Without protection, balanced accuracy moves by no more than 0.03 anywhere from layer 5 to the last layer. Moving the LLaVA split from layer 5 to 15, however, roughly doubles device latency and energy, and from layer 25 on the model no longer fits in the 11 GiB device.
- Under Safe-LLaVA, the full-budget attacker recovers 91-115% of the unprotected leakage. ImageProtector holds recovery to 25-69% on LLaVA, the model it was optimized for, but on Qwen 82-100% is recovered, so it barely transfers across models. PASS caps recovery at 39%, yet the HSIC test still finds significant dependence in every condition.
- Credibility of the metrics: results are means and standard deviations over five seeds, auxiliary and target images are disjoint, and each defense is first shown to meet its own objective (refusal rate, face verification accuracy) before being compared. The numbers are not suspiciously strong; they separate the defenses in a way that is easy to interpret.

#### Comment
- From a mobile standpoint, this is a quantitative rebuttal to the design argument that "it runs on the device, so it is private". The finding that a deeper split only adds device cost without buying privacy is especially useful in practice.
- Limitations: the study covers face images and three demographic attributes only, with a single prompt ("Describe this image"). The device and server are emulated with two desktop GPUs rather than real hardware, and at five pages some setup details are omitted. No new defense is proposed.
- Verification of a Chinese institution: the authors are at The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, a real university, and the corresponding author's email domain (cuhk.edu.cn) matches. Only public models (LLaVA, Qwen), public datasets (FairFace, UTKFace, LFW) and released defense checkpoints are used, which makes the work fairly reproducible, although the paper gives no code link. The three baselines are representative defenses at different intervention points, and the results are not structured to look overly favorable, so the paper was included. It remains an unreviewed preprint with no venue yet.
