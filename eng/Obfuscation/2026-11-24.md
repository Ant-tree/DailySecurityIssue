# BinXSight: Fast Binary Program Summarization via Hierarchical Modular Decomposition
> arXiv preprint (not peer reviewed; appears to be a submission in ACM journal format). ShanghaiTech University, Zhejiang University, Tencent Security Keen Lab, University of Glasgow
> 2026

When reverse engineering a stripped binary, analysts want a quick picture of what the whole program does without reading thousands of functions. Existing LLM-based binary summarization mostly works one function at a time, missing interactions and calling context, and the results are hard to merge into a program-level summary. BinXSight represents a binary as a "binary graph" made of four graphs, decomposes it hierarchically into functional modules, summarizes only the most significant ones, and combines the module summaries MapReduce-style into a program summary.

### Contribution
- A binary representation, the binary graph, that captures dependencies from several perspectives, not just calls.
- Following the modular design principle (high cohesion, loose coupling), functions are grouped into modules and modules that do not drive core behavior, such as error handling, are filtered out, improving both speed and quality.
- The evaluation covers malware as well as benign programs (including gh0st, Mirai and WannaCry variants), and a case study on recent malware shows practical use.

### Evaluation
- Data: 110 binaries from 92 C/C++ projects (networking, remote-control malware, ransomware and more). Duplicate functions were filtered with hashes of normalized source code to keep test data separate from data used elsewhere.
- Baselines: summarizing functions and then merging (func-sum), a variant using the state-of-the-art function summarizer MiSum, feeding all pseudocode at once (func-code), predicting function names with SymGen and merging (sym-sum), and Malsight. Because all prior methods are function-level, the authors adapted them to program level, all on gpt-5.4.
- Metrics and results: embedding similarity (SIMILARITY) of 0.6137, 23.18% to 46.99% above the baselines, with BLEU, ROUGE-L and METEOR also ahead. The authors chose embedding similarity after checking how well each metric correlates with human judgments. A binary with about 3,429 functions took 24.3 seconds on average, one with 59,173 functions took 3.8 minutes, and the total API cost was $78.1.
- User study: 15 reverse engineers with at least seven years of experience and 12 security PhD students compared BinXSight with the best baseline in random order; the authors report IRB approval.
- Obfuscation: 40 projects obfuscated with Obfuscator-LLVM instruction substitution (SUB), bogus control flow (BCF), control-flow flattening (FLA) and all combined (ALL) lowered all four metrics, more so for BCF, FLA and ALL, though BinXSight still beat the baselines in every setting.
- Reliability: the authors adapted the baselines themselves, so those may not reflect each method's best performance, and the main metric relies on an embedding model. The improvements, in the 20% to 40% range, are not excessive.

#### Comment
- From an obfuscation standpoint, the paper offers numbers on how well LLM-based program-level reverse engineering holds up against O-LLVM. The authors leave deobfuscation out of scope and note that packed binaries are beyond static analysis. For protection design, it is worth noting that even weak obfuscation such as SUB lowers summary quality.
- Because this paper comes from Chinese institutions (ShanghaiTech University, Zhejiang University, Tencent Keen Lab), it was checked more closely.
